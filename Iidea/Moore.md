# Project Idea Summary
## 1. Feral Cat Boundary: A Humane Refuge System for Native Wildlife

This concept focuses on the ecological impact of feral and roaming cats in Australia, especially their threat to native birds, reptiles, frogs, and small mammals. Instead of designing a harmful cat deterrent, the project would create a humane, context-aware boundary system that helps protect a small wildlife refuge zone.

The system could use motion sensors, light sensors, pressure sensors, and possibly camera-based AI recognition to detect whether a cat-like animal is approaching a protected area. In response, it could activate non-harmful deterrents, close a small refuge entrance, adjust a physical barrier, or alert a human caretaker.

The design would be framed as a multi-species boundary negotiation system. It considers native animals as vulnerable stakeholders, cats as non-human actors requiring ethical treatment, and humans as caretakers responsible for maintaining safer ecological conditions.

## 2. MycoSense: A Living Microclimate Companion for Fungi

This concept focuses on fungi, mushroom cultivation, and fungal microhabitats. Since one group member has access to someone who grows mushrooms, this idea has strong potential for real-world research, interviews, and site-based observation.

The system would monitor environmental conditions such as temperature, humidity, substrate moisture, light, and air movement. It would then respond through non-screen feedback such as misting, ventilation, lighting, or physical opening and closing mechanisms.

The project would not simply be a mushroom-growing device. It would be framed as a more-than-human system that supports fungal life, mycelium development, microbial relationships, and human care practices. This gives the project strong ecological and intellectual depth.

## 3. Native Bee Heat Refuge: An Ambient Support Device for Pollinators

This concept responds to threats faced by Australian native bees, including habitat loss, climate stress, changing flowering seasons, and urban environmental pressure.

The system would monitor heat, humidity, light, and nearby insect activity. When stressful conditions are detected, it could provide shade, adjust airflow, signal water or habitat needs, or activate subtle ambient feedback for human caretakers.

The enclosure could be designed as a native bee hotel, flower-inspired structure, tree-bark module, or urban garden installation. This idea is visually strong, technically achievable, and easy to demonstrate in a final presentation.

## 4. HollowWatch: A Smart Artificial Hollow for Urban Wildlife

This concept focuses on Australian animals that depend on tree hollows, such as possums, gliders, parrots, microbats, and insects. Natural hollows are often lost due to land clearing and the removal of mature trees, so artificial hollows and nest boxes are used as habitat support.

The system would monitor internal temperature, humidity, occupancy, light, and movement. It could respond by adjusting ventilation, warning human caretakers of overheating, or recording signs of animal use.

This project has a professional conservation-tool feel and could be packaged as a smart habitat-monitoring system rather than a decorative nest box.

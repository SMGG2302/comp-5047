# EcoGuardian – UV Monitoring for Australian Ecosystems

## Background

Australia experiences some of the highest levels of UV radiation in the world. UV exposure can vary significantly depending on season, time of day, weather conditions, location, and surrounding vegetation.

High levels of UV radiation can potentially affect Australian ecosystems, including animals, insects, and plants. For example, excessive UV exposure may affect animal skin and eyes, influence animal and insect behaviour, affect sensitive amphibians, and cause stress or damage to vegetation.

## Project Idea

**EcoGuardian** is a pervasive computing project that focuses on monitoring UV exposure and environmental conditions in Australian ecosystems.

The main idea is to observe how UV levels change in different environments and explore how these conditions may affect local wildlife, insects, and vegetation.

Rather than focusing on only one species, the project could consider different ecological environments and investigate how organisms experience different levels of UV exposure in their habitats.

## Visual Concept

For the physical appearance of the project, we could take inspiration from the image of a small plant growing from a boot, similar to the plant imagery in *WALL-E*.

The plant could represent nature and ecological recovery, while the overall concept of **EcoGuardian** represents technology quietly monitoring and protecting the surrounding ecosystem.

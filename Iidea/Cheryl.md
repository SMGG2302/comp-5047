
## 1. Native Plant Stress Communicator  

This focuses on environmental stress experienced by native Australian plants, such as drought, heat, and excessive sunlight. Sensors monitor soil moisture, temperature, humidity, and light conditions, while the system translates plant stress into non-screen feedback such as light, sound, or physical movement. Rather than simply helping humans care for plants, the system gives plants a way to communicate their environmental needs and become active participants in the interaction.



## 2. Cat–Wildlife Guardian  

Feral cats have a significant impact on Australia's native animals and threaten over 200 nationally threatened species (https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/invasive-species/feral-animals-australia/feral-cats/feral-cat-taskforce). This topic addresses predation risks between domestic or stray cats and native Australian wildlife. Motion sensors, cameras, and AI-based image recognition can detect animals entering backyards, parks, or bushland edges. By combining species recognition with time and environmental context, the system estimates potential wildlife risk and responds through non-harmful interventions such as light, sound, or owner notifications.



## 3. Wildlife-Friendly Adaptive Lighting  

Australian official documents (https://www.dcceew.gov.au/campaign/light-pollution/ecological-communities) clearly indicate that artificial light at night affects insects, bats, frogs, birds and terrestrial mammals, and may alter predatory relationships, foraging, and migration behaviors. Unlike conventional smart lights that respond only to human presence, the system also considers ambient light and wildlife activity. It can provide sufficient illumination when people are nearby while reducing brightness or lighting duration when nocturnal animals are active, creating a more balanced shared environment.



## 4. Invasive Plant Spread Guardian  

When bushwalking, mud on the soles of shoes and plant seeds may be carried to another area. A trail-side device can use cameras, moisture sensing, and AI image recognition to detect mud, seeds, or plant material attached to footwear. When a potential spread risk is identified, the system provides physical prompts such as light, sound, or cleaning instructions before the user enters a sensitive ecological area.
